# Mid-Autumn Festival: Speaker Notes

International Celebration, 1st grade · Nov 6 · about 5 minutes of slides, then the lantern craft

**Bring:** real mooncakes (display only, keep wrapped) · 3D-printed mooncake models to pass around · paper lantern kits

**Deck:** https://weilu.github.io/mid-autumn-festival/

**Controls:** click, → or space = next · ← = back · `f` = full screen · on slide 5, click a kid or press **1–4** to reveal a country

| # | Slide | Time |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | Welcome | 0:15 |
| 2 | What Is It? | 0:45 |
| 3 | Why & How Do We Celebrate? | 1:30 |
| 4 | Who Celebrates? | 0:30 |
| 5 | Same Holiday, Different Names | 1:00 |

## 1. Welcome

> "Hi everyone! I'm Mia's mom. Today we're going to learn about a fall holiday celebrated in Chinese culture and by many Asian countries. It's about three things: the **harvest**, **family**, and the **moon**!"

---

## 2. What Is It?

> "Mid-Autumn means the **middle of fall**. Watch the moon. It gets bigger… and bigger… and rounder… until it's a **giant full moon**!"
>
> "The festival is on the night when the moon is the biggest and roundest of the whole year. This year it was **September 25th**."

---

## 3. Why & How Do We Celebrate?

**Click 1 · Harvest**
> "Long ago, most families were farmers. In the fall they picked their rice and fruit. When the work was done, they celebrated and said **thank you for the food**."

**Click 2 · Families Together**
> "This is the most important part. Families travel from far away to be **together**: grandmas, grandpas, moms, dads and kids. Everyone comes home."

> "Does that sound like a holiday we have here?" (Thanksgiving!)

**Click 3 · Mooncakes & Lanterns**
> "And how do families celebrate? They eat **mooncakes**, which are round like the full moon, and kids carry **glowing lanterns** at night, like this red one and this star one from Vietnam."

**Mooncake show-and-tell** (real mooncakes + 3D-printed models)

> Hold up a real mooncake: "Here's a real mooncake! See the pretty pattern pressed on top? Inside there's a sweet filling, and some even have a golden egg yolk in the middle, like a little full moon!"

> Hand out the 3D-printed mooncakes: "These are pretend mooncakes you can touch. Look closely and feel the pattern with your fingers. Is it round like the moon? Then gently pass it to your neighbor."

- The real mooncakes are **for looking only**: no food is allowed at the event, so keep them wrapped on the display table.
- Passing takes time. Keep going to slide 4 while the models go around, and have a helper collect them before the latern craft.


---

## 4. Who Celebrates?

> "Who celebrates? Families in **China**, **Taiwan**, **Vietnam**, **Korea**, **Japan**, **Singapore** and **Malaysia**, and Asian families all around the world, including right here in Virginia!"

**Click** → the bunny hops and asks:
> "**Raise your hand if your family celebrates!**"

Count or acknowledge the hands.

---

## 5. Same Holiday, Different Names

| Kid | Country | Name | Say |
|-----|---------|------|-----|
| 1 | China | Zhōngqiū Jié | jong-chyoh jyeh |
| 2 | Vietnam | Tết Trung Thu | tet choong too |
| 3 | Korea | Chuseok | choo-sock |
| 4 | Japan | Tsukimi | tsoo-kee-mee |

> "Different countries have different names for this holiday."

Read each name once (point at each kid).

**Click** → the game starts: the title becomes **"Which country is it?"** and the countries turn into **?**

Choose one version:

### Version A · Two volunteers

1. > "I need two helpers!" Pick two kids and bring them up.
2. Helper 1 secretly **picks one kid** on the screen and **says that name out loud** (they can read the "Say:" line).
3. Helper 2 **guesses which country** it is.
4. Click that kid (or press its number) to **reveal**. The country pops back in and the kid jumps.
5. If there's time, swap roles or bring up a new pair for another name.

> "Great job! Let's give our helpers a big clap!"

### Version B · I say it, the class answers

1. > "I'm going to say a name. If you know which country it's from, raise your hand!"
2. Say one name clearly, e.g. "**Chuseok**!"
3. Call on a kid to answer.
4. Click that kid (or press its number) to **reveal**.
5. Repeat for as many names as time allows. Suggested order: **Tsukimi → Chuseok → Tết Trung Thu → Zhōngqiū Jié** (easiest to hear first).

Tip for both versions: the clothes are hints (the conical hat is Vietnam, the kimono is Japan).

## 6. The Rabbit on the Moon

> "Here's a secret about the moon. Look closely… do you see a **rabbit**?"

This is a real photo of the moon. Give the kids a few seconds to search.

**Click 1** → the rabbit outline appears on the moon.
> "There it is! See its long ears up here, and it's standing next to a pot. People have seen this rabbit on the real moon for a long, long time, and they tell stories about it. Next time there's a full moon, look up and find it!"

**Click 2** → China card
> "In China, there's a story about a kind lady named **Chang'e**. She drank a magic potion and floated up, up, up to the moon, and her pet rabbit, the **Jade Rabbit**, went with her! The rabbit is still up there making **magic medicine**."

**Click 3** → Japan & Korea card (the rabbit pounds rice cakes)
> "In **Japan and Korea**, people say the rabbit on the moon is **making rice cakes** instead. Yum!"

---

## 7. Let's Make Paper Lanterns!

> "Kids carry lanterns on Mid-Autumn night, so now **you** get to make one!"

**Click 1 · Pop it open**
> "First, hold the flat lantern and gently pull it open, like this, until it's round."

**Click 2 · Put the wire in**
> "Next, take the wire and slide it **inside** the lantern. It holds the lantern open so it stays round."

**Click 3 · Twist it on**
> "Then take the little light and **twist** it. Look, it turns on!"

**Click 4 · Hang the light**
> "Last, hang the light on the little **hook** inside the top. Now your lantern glows!"

All the lanterns on screen light up and **"Happy Mid-Autumn Festival! 中秋节快乐"** appears.

> "Happy Mid-Autumn Festival! **Zhōng Qiū Jié Kuài Lè!**"

Leave this slide up during the craft so helpers can point to the steps. Finished lanterns go into the kids' Ziploc bags.
